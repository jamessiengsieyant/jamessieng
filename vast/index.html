Topic 1

Capitalize or Expense?

Two real situations where I had to make, or defend, that call.

Example 1 — ForensisGroup, Salesforce

The technical spine: a Salesforce subscription is a Cloud Computing Arrangement under ASC 350-40 (as amended by ASU 2018-15) — the subscription fee itself is never an asset, it's always a straight period expense, like rent. The real judgment call lives in the implementation costs: configuration, customization, and testing get capitalized as a prepaid asset and expensed ratably over the contract term; training and data conversion get expensed immediately, even though they feel like "setup work" too.

CapitalizeConfiguration, customization, scripting, testing
ExpenseSubscription/hosting fees, training, data conversion

Topic 2

The First 90 Days at Vast

30 days

Map the chart of accounts and capitalization threshold. Learn how Ramp is configured — category mappings, AI-coding accuracy, dimensions in use. Shadow month-end close, flag where manual reclasses happen most.

60 days

Tighten GL coding rules for the recurring judgment calls — CCA/SaaS implementation costs, capex thresholds. Help document the capitalization policy crisply. Start quantifying the manual-reclass problem.

90 days

Pilot the idea below on a narrow slice of spend, with a before/after on reclass volume. Bring a lightweight business case, not just a demo.

The idea

Route the capitalize-vs-expense decision to the point of transaction entry in Ramp, not to close.

AI-assisted coding in Ramp is tuned for speed and general category accuracy — not purpose-built to catch the two calls that create rework later: an invoice crossing the capitalization threshold, or a SaaS vendor bill blending capitalizable implementation work with costs that must stay expensed on the same invoice.

Transaction hits Ramp
↓ AI category + rules
Below threshold, routine → auto-code as expense
Above threshold, tangible asset → route to capex review queue
CCA / SaaS implementation vendor → split: capitalize config & testing, expense training & data conversion
↓
Approved coding syncs to NetSuite

Small build, immediate payoff: fewer reclass journal entries at close, a cleaner audit trail, and a policy that used to live in someone's head now lives in the system.
